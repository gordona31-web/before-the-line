Nashville · October 13, 2025

Thousands of people, one name on the marquee

Gordon

In October 2025 my school took a senior trip to Nashville. I had no idea Laufey was playing there that weekend until we got there. Walking past the Predators' arena one day, I saw thousands of her fans lined up to pack a sold-out show. It was crazy to me, thinking back to when I first heard “Valentine” and she was barely on anyone's radar.

Two days later she played the first of two sold-out nights at Madison Square Garden. When I first heard her in late 2022, she had about 3 million monthly listeners and had never charted on the Billboard 200. This page traces what happened in between and why it worked, using archived Spotify and TikTok data, my own Chartmetric pulls and the Laufey Fan Survey (84 respondents, October 2026). At the end, I lay out what I'd look for when scouting the next one.

The curve

Listeners spiked and settled. The fanbase kept climbing.

Her listener count swings hard every December, when her holiday songs go into heavy rotation. Over two weeks in December 2024 she went from 27 million to 43 million listeners, and on December 26, 2025 she passed 47 million, her all-time high. Those spikes fade within about a month. The rest of the year tells a steadier story: about 13 million through most of 2024, about 15 million in spring 2025, then a step up to about 24 million once A Matter of Time and the arena tour arrived, settling at 21.7 million today. Through those dips she sold out Radio City, the Royal Albert Hall and the Hollywood Bowl, then sold more than 250,000 arena tickets before her next album was even out. Listener counts track the current moment. Followers, sales and tickets track how many people are actually committed.

Five growth steps

Six years, and each step built on the last

2020–21

A seed audience

Home from Berklee during the pandemic, she posted a cello-and-vocal cover of “It Could Happen to You” on Instagram and went from about 1,000 followers to about 10,000. Her debut single “Street by Street” went to #1 on Icelandic radio. While she had only two self-released singles out, she took meetings with labels and managers without signing anything, which she later called “a second education.”

~10K Instagram followers · 100K streams in month one

Why it worked. A 20-year-old singing jazz standards over her own cello was something people hadn't seen, it landed while everyone was stuck at home, and she kept showing up: about four TikToks a week, plus a livestream every Sunday.

Fan survey 10 of 84 fans were already listening by 2021, several hooked by early covers like “I Wish You Love” and “Love to Keep Me Warm.”

2022

The first real growth

V of BTS shared “Like the Movies” in February. “Valentine” came out on Valentine's Day after an unfinished TikTok clip took off. In April she signed a global deal with AWAL and kept her masters. Her debut album missed the Billboard 200 entirely, but she ended the year as Spotify's most-streamed jazz artist.

~3M monthly listeners · ~700K TikTok followers by year end

Why it worked. She tried songs out on fans before finishing them, and from April she went from about one TikTok every other day to more than one a day. Her TikTok following nearly doubled in seven months, from 360,000 to 694,000.

Fan survey Most of the 21 fans who started by 2022 were hooked by the debut-era songs: “Valentine,” “Falling Behind,” “Let You Break My Heart Again” and Everything I Know About Love.

2023

“From the Start” and Bewitched: the defining moment

This is the moment that blew her up. “From the Start” came out in May as the first single from Bewitched, hit 1 million streams in under a day and took over TikTok. By July it was on Spotify's US and global charts, and it is still her biggest song by far, at 1.3 billion streams. The album landed in September and set Spotify's record for a jazz album's first day with 5.7 million streams, on the same day as Olivia Rodrigo's GUTS. Between February and September her monthly listeners went from 3,194,077 to 13,484,506, more than four times over.

3.2M to 13.5M listeners · Billboard 200 #23 debut · first-ever chart entry

Why it worked. She kept posting about 1.6 TikToks a day through the whole run. An official sped-up version of “From the Start” came out in late July, right as the song reached the US chart. The weekend Bewitched came out, A Very Laufey Day gave fans something to do, and they made more than 40,000 TikToks.

Fan survey 2023 was the biggest year for new fans: 33 of 84 started listening then. Across all years, 35 of 84 named “From the Start” or Bewitched as what got them hooked, more than any other song or album. The most common way 2023 fans found her was a friend or family member (11 of 33). TikTok brought in 5.

2024

Listeners dip, rooms grow

She won her first Grammy in February, the youngest winner ever in Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album. The Goddess deluxe pushed Bewitched to a new peak, and the whole tour sold out in minutes. She played a sold-out Hollywood Bowl with the LA Phil, which became a concert film.

Radio City: 11,894 tickets over two nights · Hollywood Bowl ~17,000

Why it worked. She could afford to post less. Touring, she dropped to about one TikTok every two days by late 2024, and her following still grew from 5.8 million to 7.3 million in about five months. The audience she built online was now showing up in person.

Fan survey Half of the 14 fans who started in 2024 (7 of 14) were hooked by “From the Start” or Bewitched, a year or more after release. The catalog kept recruiting after the spike.

2025–26

An arena artist

A Matter of Time debuted at #4 with 99,000 units, more than four times her previous album's first week. A fan-made remix brought “From the Start” back to the global chart two years after release. A 70-show arena tour ran from September 2025 to September 2026, with a second Grammy along the way. Four days after the tour ended, she signed with Interscope/Verve.

250K+ North American tickets sold before the album came out

Why it worked. When fans turned a remix of “From the Start” into a dance trend, she joined in instead of ignoring it. For the album release she brought A Very Laufey Day onto TikTok LIVE, streamed from Los Angeles to an account that had 8.8 million followers by then.

Fan survey 14 of 84 fans started in 2025 or 2026, hooked by A Matter of Time, “Lover Girl” and “Madwoman.” Six years in, she's still bringing in new fans.

Why it worked

She built the audience one video at a time

The covers: something nobody had seen

When Berklee sent everyone home in March 2020, Laufey gave herself a job.

“I'm just going to write and post as many videos online of me singing jazz standards as I can. I'll just see where it takes me.”Laufey to Billboard, January 2024

The first ones to catch on were “It Could Happen to You,” sung over her own cello, and “I Wish You Love.” I think they worked for three reasons at once. The combination was new: as she put it, people watching were thinking, “Why is this girl, this young woman, playing cello and singing?” The songs were already proven: standards have lasted 70 years because they're great songs, and her manager points out that almost everyone has some memory of jazz or classical music, even if they never chose it. And the timing was perfect. Everyone was home with time to watch, and TikTok was the most downloaded app of 2020. She called it “the perfect storm.”

The volume: more than one TikTok a day

The archived profiles show how much she actually posted. From April 2022 to October 2023, the stretch that covers “Valentine,” her debut album and “From the Start,” she posted 814 videos in 547 days, about one and a half a day. Her TikTok following went from 360,000 to 3.1 million over the same stretch. Plenty of those videos weren't songs. They were bits with her twin sister Junia, reactions to fan videos and pieces of her day, which made the account feel like a person you were following. That shows up in the data on this page: she has 2.4 TikTok followers for every video made with her music, more than almost any artist her size. Once the arena tours started she slowed to about one video every two days, and by then the fanbase was carrying itself.

In my capstone I laid out the playbook U.S. labels run on TikTok: build a hook that grabs people in the first three seconds, send the song to influencers, post the same clip on every platform, then pitch Spotify's playlist editors. The whole chain is designed to create momentum that looks organic. Laufey built the real thing herself, on her own account, years before she signed with a major label.

The livestreams: a jazz club in your bedroom

In 2021 she was going live on Instagram at least once a week, with a standing show every Sunday at 6 p.m. called “Lullabies with Laufey,” where she just sang jazz songs. She compared it to a jazz club: small, spontaneous, the performer right in front of you.

“I feel so much more connected to my fans… I'm just so honored to have people watching me practice every week.”Laufey to From the Intercom, April 2021

The streams also worked as a test run. How fans reacted told her which songs were worth taking into the studio. “I Wish You Love” started as a cover she shared online and ended up on her first EP. “Valentine” started as an unfinished TikTok. Four years later she was doing the same thing on a much bigger stage: A Very Laufey Day 2025 was streamed on TikTok LIVE from Los Angeles.

That lines up with a study I used in my capstone. Mallard (2023) followed independent artists' TikToks and Spotify numbers and found that the ones who turned the most TikTok followers into Spotify listeners were the ones who told fans about upcoming music and made it interactive. Weekly livestreams and unfinished clips do both.

The intimacy: she does it herself

She runs her own accounts. Before releases she has posted the sheet music, asked musician fans to learn the song without hearing it, and reposted their versions. She runs a book club. The weekend Bewitched came out she drove around LA rolling down her window to say hi to fans dressed like her. When she played Rockwood Music Hall in New York after lockdown and heard there was a line outside, she was confused, because coming from symphony concerts, “nobody lines up like that.” That was the first sign that the people watching her online would show up in person. By 2025 it was two sold-out nights at Madison Square Garden.

Fan survey TikTok was one of the three main ways fans found her (18 of 84, tied with friends or family and one behind YouTube at 19), and Instagram brought in 10 more. That's a third of respondents (28 of 84) coming in through her two main accounts.

The result: followers who stay

TikTok grew fastest in 2023, the year of “From the Start,” going from about 950,000 followers in March to 3.1 million that October. Instagram's biggest jump came a year later. It went from about 2 million in January 2024 to about 4.4 million by August and around 5 million by late October, the year she won her first Grammy and toured Bewitched. Followers don't swing with the seasons the way her listener count does. Both lines keep climbing, with no December spikes, to 11.1 million on TikTok and 9.6 million on Instagram today.

After the breakout

What she built after the spike

My capstone stopped at the spike. It measured how much each artist's Spotify listeners grew during their TikTok moment, and Laufey's grew 322% between February and September 2023. This page picks up where that left off.

Plenty of artists have a 2023 like hers and then fade. What makes Laufey different is what happened next. Her listener count grew about 1.6× after the breakout. Every measure of committed fans grew much faster: the rooms she sells out, the albums people buy in the first week, the people who follow her on TikTok.

The viral moment got people in the door. What she built afterwards is why they stayed.

May–September 2023 · “From the Start” to Bewitched

The moment it clicked

Then “From the Start” came out, and to me that was her first big mainstream moment. I loved it from the first listen. I was following her on Instagram and saw her promoting a new album for September. It was dropping the same day as Olivia Rodrigo's GUTS, so I was really excited for that night of new music. Looking back, it was one of the best weeks for new music in recent memory, with Mitski's The Land Is Inhospitable and So Are We coming a week later. That was three really important albums for 19-year-old me, all in a few weeks.

At first Bewitched got a little overshadowed by how much I loved GUTS. On my first listen, “Dreamer” stood out for its jazzy elements and “Lovesick” for its big chorus. Over the next couple of weeks I kept coming back to it, until I liked every song on the album. That's what makes it so impressive to me. It came out the same day as one of the biggest pop albums of the year and could easily have been buried, and people still kept finding their way back to it, just like I did.

“From the Start” has 1.3 billion Spotify streams, more than twice her next-biggest song.

Previous jazz record · 1.1MBewitched, day one · 5.7M

Bewitched drew 5.7 million Spotify streams on its first day, the biggest ever for a jazz album. The previous record was 1.1 million.

It did that on the same day as GUTS, which debuted at #1 on the Billboard 200 with 302,000 units. Bewitched still entered at #23, her first time on the chart.

2023–24 · The deep dive

Back to the beginning

After that album I was hooked. I went back and listened to everything from the beginning, and I loved the deluxe that came out months later, which had two of her best songs, “Goddess” and “Bored.”

51.5% of her monthly listeners also follow her on Spotify (11.2M of 21.7M), against 36.5% for the median artist her size (the tick). Following is a deliberate step that casual listeners rarely take. The artists level with her include blink-182 and Elvis Presley, acts with decades of fans behind them.

When the deluxe came out, seven months after release, Bewitched climbed to a new Billboard 200 peak of #18.

Number one artist of the year

Laufey ended up being my number one artist of 2024, and I picked up several of her records for my collection.

Her Record Store Day pressings grew from 4,200 copies in 2024 to 17,000 in 2026, and her 2026 live album was the #5 RSD exclusive in the US.

October 2025 · The arena

Bridgestone Arena, sold out

Seeing that line in Nashville is when it really hit me how big she had gotten. Getting to watch her grow the whole way was amazing, and I loved being a part of it.

Her biggest headline room grew about ten times between 2023 and 2025. Her listeners grew less than twice over the same period.

She sold 1.8 North American arena tickets for every 100 monthly listeners, before the album was even out.

The funnel shows up in the fan survey too. The longer someone has been a fan, the further along they are. 70% of fans who started by 2022 have seen her live and 74% own a vinyl or CD. For fans who started in 2025 or 2026, it's 14% and 29%, and more than a third haven't done any of those things yet. Converting a new fan takes time, so the fans she gained in 2025 are the next tour's audience.

How their paths split

All four broke through on one TikTok-driven song while their audiences were still small. None of those songs was a big radio hit at first; each one spread because people used it in their own videos. After that, their paths split. Lizzy McAlpine is the closest twin. She went to Berklee, released on AWAL and went viral in early 2023 at about 3.6 million listeners, with an even bigger spike. Today she has 15.5 million listeners but only 2.1 million Spotify followers, and 6.3 million TikTok videos use her songs while 1.3 million people follow her there, so her songs reached more people than she did. She also signed with a major label right after the spike, while Laufey stayed independent until she was playing arenas. Noah Kahan turned his moment into the largest audience of the group, with 35 million listeners, a #1 album and more than a million stadium tickets, and he had major-label backing the whole way. His audience is the widest, but a smaller share of it follows him than Laufey's. Megan Moroney started from the smallest base and sells the most tickets for her size, with two sold-out nights at Bridgestone Arena on 8.4 million listeners. Country's radio and touring culture helps turn listeners into ticket buyers, which shows how much genre shapes these numbers.

Of the four, Laufey's fans seem the most attached to her as a person. She is the only one of the four with more TikTok followers than TikTok videos using her official sounds.

Artists her size

Her listeners follow her everywhere

“Her size” here means every artist on Spotify within 15% of her monthly listeners: 18.4M to 25.0M on October 23, 2026. That's 217 other artists, from Gucci Mane to Shania Twain. For each one I pulled Spotify, Instagram and TikTok followers from Chartmetric on the same day and divided them by monthly listeners.

Why these 217. Follow rates change with audience size, so comparing artists with about the same number of listeners takes size out of the picture. Within 15% was a balance between fair and big enough: within 5% there are only about 60 artists, too few to trust a median, and my first, wider cut of 16 to 28 million pulled in audiences of a different size. The group is every artist on kworb's Spotify ranking in that range on the same day, with no hand-picking, which is why Elvis, BLACKPINK and Indian film singers sit next to her. Almost no jazz acts are near her size, and a few breakout peers would have been a group I chose myself. The trade-off is a group that mixes genres, eras and countries.

Where she stands out most

The biggest gap is on TikTok. The median artist her size has 6 TikTok followers for every 100 monthly listeners, and Laufey has 51, about eight times as many. Only 14 artists in the group are higher, and most are K-pop groups, Latin pop stars and rappers like BLACKPINK, Camilo and Lil Nas X, whose fanbases live on social media. More people follow her than use her songs. For most artists, far more people use their songs in TikToks than follow them: the median artist her size has 0.13 TikTok followers for every video made with their music. Laufey has 2.4, and only LE SSERAFIM is higher. For her audience size, her songs actually show up in fewer videos than most artists', but far more people follow her. One caveat: Chartmetric only counts videos that use an artist's official sounds, so sped-up versions, remixes and fan-uploaded clips are left out. That undercounts every artist in the group, so treat these video numbers as a floor. On Instagram she has 44 followers per 100 listeners, nearly three times the median of 16. Instagram is where I first saw her promoting Bewitched, and a lot of her listeners went looking for the person behind the music. Spotify is where the gap is smallest, because Spotify followers build up over a whole career and catalog acts like Led Zeppelin, Bob Marley and Luis Miguel get a head start. Laufey has only been releasing music since 2020 and is already higher than 7 in 10 artists her size.

Fan Intensity Index

How committed are her fans?

Monthly listeners measure how many people heard an artist recently. These three signals try to measure how many of them actually care: do they follow the artist, keep coming back to the music and listen past the biggest hit? Each signal is a ratio rather than a raw count, so a small artist can score as high as a big one. Laufey is compared with the same 217 artists her size as in the section above.

How the score works Fan Intensity = (follow rate + listening depth + catalog breadth) ÷ 3

The index averages three measures. Follow rate adds up an artist's Spotify, Instagram and TikTok followers and divides by their Spotify monthly listeners, because following is a step a casual listener rarely takes on any platform. Listening depth is Last.fm plays divided by Last.fm listeners, or how many times the average listener has played the artist. Catalog breadth is the share of an artist's Spotify streams that come from outside their top three songs, which shows whether fans are into the artist or into one hit. Each measure is turned into a percentile within the group, from 0 for the lowest artist to 100 for the highest, so one platform's huge numbers can't drown out the others. The score is the average of the three. Every input was pulled for all 218 artists on October 23, 2026, from Chartmetric, Last.fm and kworb.

What the score shows, and what it misses

She ranks 10th of 213, and her two strongest signals are the most personal ones: people follow her (90th percentile) and replay her (95th). Most of the artists above her are rap, K-pop and Latin acts known for devoted fanbases, like YoungBoy Never Broke Again, BLACKPINK, LE SSERAFIM and Lil Peep, and she's the only artist in the top 10 whose music comes out of jazz.

Every input has a blind spot. Followers and plays build up over a whole career, and breadth rewards big catalogs, so all three lean toward veterans, which works against Laufey, who has only been releasing music since 2020. Last.fm users lean Western, indie and alternative, but Spotify backs up her depth score: her listeners stream her about 7.4 times a month, against 3.2 for the median artist her size. Last.fm was matched by artist name, so a few artists could be mixed up with someone of the same name. I also left two signals out on purpose. I tested live demand, using Bandsintown followers and reported ticket sales, but both mostly reward decades of touring, and real ticket numbers only exist for the biggest tours, so neither is fair across 213 artists. Her live numbers are better shown than scored: Billboard has her at 319,000 tickets across 32 shows from October 2025 to March 2026. I dropped hold, how much of an artist's peak audience is still listening, because her peak was a two-week holiday surge. And the index can't see what fans do offline. Following and streaming are free, while buying a ticket, a record or a book club membership costs something. None of that is in the index, which is why the fan survey below matters.

Fan survey The survey adds a signal no tracker has: what fans actually do. 70 of the 84 respondents (83%) have done at least one of these: seen her live (51%), bought a vinyl or CD (50%), joined her book club or a fan community (45%), or bought merch (42%). 14 have done all four.

Beyond the numbers

Opening the door to jazz

Gordon

She's gotten so many young people into jazz, including me. Listening to her, and watching interviews where she talks about the jazz legends who shaped her, got me into jazz itself. I started listening to Ella Fitzgerald and Chet Baker, and to classic standards like “Misty.”

“I always had this goal and dream of bringing jazz music back to my generation.”Laufey to GRAMMY.com, September 2023

She has also said jazz has so many barriers to entry for young listeners, and that she wants to take them down (Grammy.com, 2023). She names Chet Baker as one of her biggest influences and recommends him to friends who are new to jazz. Even her first viral moment in 2020 was a jazz standard, “It Could Happen to You.”

Jazz at pop scale

She has put up pop-sized numbers in a genre that rarely sees them. She was Spotify's most-streamed jazz artist of 2022, and Bewitched drew 5.7 million streams on its first day, the biggest ever for a jazz album on Spotify, against a previous record of 1.1 million. Bewitched was Billboard's #1 jazz album of 2024, and A Matter of Time was the highest Billboard 200 debut for a jazz album since 2018. She is also paying it forward. The Laufey Foundation, launched in May 2025, funds youth orchestras, including Carnegie Hall's NYO Jazz, and gets $1 from every ticket on her arena tour, and she set up a $100,000 scholarship at Syracuse in 2024. She has said the support she got as a child led directly to her career. Her new label brings it full circle: Verve Records was started in 1956 around signing Ella Fitzgerald, whose Cole Porter songbook defined the label, and in September 2026 Laufey signed with Interscope and Verve. Listener-overlap data now groups her with jazz legends like Ella Fitzgerald, Laurindo Almeida and Norah Jones, and with younger jazz-leaning artists like Samara Joy, Mei Semones, Sienna Spiro and Stacey Ryan.

The big totals barely moved. Chet Baker's biggest one-year jump, +24%, came in 2023, the year Laufey broke, while Miles Davis grew 9% that year. That fits the idea of a Laufey effect, but Miles also grew over the full period, and Ella Fitzgerald stayed flat. Legacy catalogs this large hide individual stories like mine. So I asked fans directly.

Laufey Fan Survey · 84 respondents · October 21–26, 2026

Did Laufey get you into jazz?

62% of respondents listen to more jazz because of her (52 of 84), and 25 said “a lot more.”

85% of the 61 fans who weren't already jazz listeners say she got them into it.

69% of fans under 21 say she got them into jazz. Only 7 of the 45 already listened to it.

Chet Baker came up more than anyone, named by 20 of the 47 fans who listed what they'd discovered. Ella Fitzgerald was second with 16. That's the same path I took, and it lines up with the Spotify data above: Chet Baker's biggest one-year jump came in 2023, the year she broke.

The effect is strongest with the youngest fans. Of the 14 respondents aged 16 and under, 9 say she got them into jazz, and only one was already a jazz listener. Fans who got into jazz through her are also more committed to her: 62% have seen her live, compared with 34% of everyone else.

In their words

Respondents' own answers point the same way. One wrote, “She inspired me to take a jazz class.” Another said, “Her cover of ‘Misty’ was definitely the first one I listened to.” A third is “now more interested in the jazz and bossa nova artists Laufey acknowledged as an influence, particularly Julie London and Luiz Bonfá.” And one fan who isn't there yet put it simply: “I don't listen to jazz… YET.”

Methodology: anonymous seven-question online questionnaire, distributed on Reddit, Instagram and through personal networks, October 21–26, 2026 (n = 84). Respondents self-selected, so the sample leans toward engaged fans and doesn't represent all of her listeners. Discovery was spread out: YouTube (19), friends or family (18), TikTok (18), Spotify (13) and Instagram (10). Quotes are lightly trimmed.

What I'd look for

Lessons for scouting the next one

  1. Notice the spike, then watch what comes after

    “From the Start” is what put her on the map. The signals that showed she would last came after it: how fast rooms sold out, how many listeners followed her, and how much of an album debut came from people buying it. Discovery wasn't one channel either. In the fan survey, YouTube, friends or family and TikTok were within one vote of each other (23%, 21% and 21%), and for fans under 21, friends and family came first.

  2. Owning a small category builds credibility fast

    She was #1 on the jazz charts and won in a less crowded Grammy category. Being the clear leader of an underserved lane bought her press and prestige that a crowded pop lane would not have. It also pulls fans deeper: 62% of surveyed fans now listen to more jazz because of her.

  3. Consistency comes before the hit

    Before “From the Start” broke, she had already posted hundreds of TikToks and was averaging more than one a day. The breakout didn't come out of nowhere. It landed on an account people were already in the habit of watching. When I scout, I'd look at how often an artist shows up over months, and treat one video popping as a reason to keep watching.

  4. Test songs in public

    “Valentine” and “Falling Behind” started as rough TikToks, and her weekly livestreams told her which songs fans wanted recorded. By the time a single came out, she already knew people wanted it. An artist who shares unfinished work and listens to the response is doing early A&R on themselves.

  5. Rituals turn listeners into fans

    A weekly livestream, a book club, A Very Laufey Day in 75+ cities, a holiday song every year, orchestra shows. Each gave fans something to do, and each repeats on a schedule. 45% of surveyed fans have joined her book club or a fan community, and five named an orchestral or live recording as what first hooked them.

  6. An indie partner can win the early years

    She didn't need a major to break. She needed a partner that provided services and let her keep ownership. That's the advantage a label like Mom+Pop can offer an artist at the stage where I'd want to find them.

Gordon

When I first found Laufey, I didn't know about the mountain of work she'd put in so that people like me could find her. I also didn't know I was getting on a train early, one that would go further than I ever expected. Through my years as a fan, it's been a joy to watch her rise. She clearly has the talent of a star, but in my opinion, what matters more is her passion and love for music, and that makes people who share that passion care. You can see it in her interviews, her covers and her performances, in how much she appreciates jazz, and in the respect she shows the artists who came before her. That can't be faked, and I think it's why so many people have grown attached to her story. Standing outside that arena in Nashville, I finally saw how many people felt the same way I did. Now I want to find the next artist like that early, before the line forms.
